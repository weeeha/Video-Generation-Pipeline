# Flesh Station

**One-line:** an orbital station corridor consumed by an occult bio-weapon — the architecture is still there underneath, but the space is finishing its turn into an organism.

## Canonical description

A derelict space-station corridor overgrown by living tissue. Ropey red-brown sinew and
thick fleshy tendrils arch across the ceiling and down both walls, closing the passage into
a wet ribbed tunnel; membranous sacs and clustered egg-like nodules bulge between them,
and long chitinous crab-like limbs jut from the walls at head height. Every surface is
slick with ichor that beads, glistens and drips. The original station survives only in
patches — pale industrial wall panelling, a rectangular pressure-door frame, metal floor
grating — swallowed at the edges by the growth. Cold desaturated grey-teal ambience against
deep maroon, liver-red and raw-pink flesh tones; the only light is dim and directional,
falling away down the corridor into black. Photoreal grimdark military sci-fi, handheld
camera, heavy film grain, wet specular highlights, deep shadow.

## Staging conventions

- Depth runs **away from camera**; the unlit far end of the corridor is where the threat is.
- Ceiling growth is always heavier than floor growth — the floor stays walkable grating.
- Emergency lighting, when used, is red and comes from **behind** the flesh, not in front.

## Don'ts

- Never clean, lit, or intact — there is no "before" version of this corridor.
- No visible gore-red saturation boost; the palette stays desaturated, the flesh reads wet
  rather than bright.
- No on-screen text, signage or readable labels (see [gotchas.md](../../../knowledge/gotchas.md) —
  Seedance text is unreliable; burn any caption in post).
- Don't let it become a cave: the station geometry — panels, door frames, grating — must
  stay legible under the growth.
