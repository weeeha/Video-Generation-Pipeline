# Warbeast

**One-line:** the squad's heavy quadruped combat walker — a sabre-toothed panther cast in
armour plate; point unit, mobile cover, and the reason anyone gets out.

## Canonical description

A heavy four-legged combat robot built in the shape of a sabre-toothed big cat — low-slung
and broad, its shoulder reaching about a standing soldier's chest, long-bodied enough to
take up most of a corridor floor. Segmented gunmetal armour plating, weathered and
scuffed, over heavy hydraulic limbs that end in three-toed claw feet. A skull-like feline
head with bared sabre fangs and a single glowing cyan optic in the eye socket; swept
ear-like antenna vanes. Two long rotary cannons are mounted along its flanks, muzzle
clusters projecting forward past the shoulders. Boxed ammunition and equipment modules sit
over the rear haunches. Orange-yellow hazard chevrons and small stencilled unit codes break
up the grey. It moves with weight — slow, deliberate, servo-driven.

Scale is the first thing to drift — the 2026-08-05 T2V baseline rendered the machine at
roughly trooper height with the wrong finish. Pair ref 2 with the wording below:

> *"Match the scale shown in Image 3: the machine is low-slung and heavy, its shoulder
> reaching about a standing soldier's chest, long-bodied and broad enough to take up most
> of the corridor floor."*

## Canonical motion phrasing

Reuse this wording — it is what keeps the walk from turning into a trot:

> *"…advances at a slow deliberate walk, one heavy foot at a time, the whole chassis
> settling on each step; hydraulics hiss, the head sweeps side to side scanning ahead."*

## Don'ts

- Never fast, never agile, never leaping — the weight is the character.
- Optic is **cyan only**; red reads as hostile and breaks the "it's ours" staging.
- No rider, no mounted crew.
- Don't let it read as an organic animal — the plating and hydraulics must stay visible.
