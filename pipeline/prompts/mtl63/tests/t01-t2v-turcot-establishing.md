One continuous five-second ultra-wide establishing shot of the abandoned Turcot Interchange in Montreal twenty-five years after technological collapse. A colossal dormant road-maintenance machine has become fused with the interchange: layered crawler tracks, gantries, cable cranes, maintenance decks, patched industrial housings, and sparse young trees rooted in accumulated soil across its upper surfaces. Floodwater covers the broken ramps below and the distant Montreal skyline is visible through cool post-rain haze. Two tiny helmeted reclaimers, seen only from behind with no faces visible, walk slowly along the wet centre roadway toward the machine to establish scale. The camera makes a restrained, perfectly stable dolly forward at chest height; no cuts, orbit, zoom, or sudden motion. Preserve grounded photorealistic live-action scale, realistic civil engineering, wet concrete, oxidized steel, muted blue-grey daylight, and a few dim amber maintenance lights. Do not make the machine move, transform, attack, glow brightly, or resemble a spaceship. No readable signs, logos, HUD, or watermark. Ambient audio only: distant wind through steel, water dripping from elevated ramps, shallow footsteps, and a low intermittent electrical hum. No dialogue and no music.
